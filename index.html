Types of Home Insurance Coverage

The right policy form depends on whether you own a house, a condo or co-op, or rent.

Policy TypeWho It's ForWhat It Covers
HO-3Single-family homeownersThe standard policy — open-peril dwelling coverage plus named-peril coverage for your belongings.
HO-5Higher-value homeownersBroader than an HO-3 — open-peril coverage for both the dwelling and your personal property, usually at replacement cost.
HO-6Condo & co-op owners"Walls-in" coverage — interior walls, personal property, liability, and any improvements you've made. The building's own policy covers the structure and common areas.
HO-4RentersCovers personal property, liability, and additional living expenses. Does not cover the building itself — that's your landlord's policy.

What a Standard Policy Covers

CoverageWhat It Protects
DwellingYour home's structure — walls, roof, foundation, built-in appliances — usually at full replacement cost.
Other StructuresDetached garage, shed, fence, or pool.
Personal PropertyFurniture, electronics, clothing, and appliances, typically 50-70% of your dwelling limit.

Medical PaymentsGuest medical bills regardless of fault, without a liability claim.

What's usually NOT covered

  • Flood damage — needs a separate policy
  • Earthquake damage — needs an endorsement
  • Gradual wear, rot, or neglect
  • Sewer & sump pump backup — add-on available
  • Home business equipment & liability
  • Jewelry, art & collectibles above sub-limits

Common Questions

Is home insurance required?

Not by law in any state we serve, but a mortgage lender will require it for the life of the loan. Skip it and your lender can force-place a policy that costs more and covers less.

Does my policy cover flood damage?

No — flood is excluded from every standard homeowners policy, in every state. If you're near water or in a FEMA flood zone, ask us about a separate flood policy.

Do I need a policy for my co-op or condo?

Yes. The building's master policy only covers the structure and common areas — an HO-6 policy covers everything from the walls in, plus your liability.

What's the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value?

Replacement cost pays what it takes to rebuild or replace with new; actual cash value subtracts depreciation. We recommend replacement cost on both dwelling and contents wherever it's available.

Common Questions

What commercial insurance is required for my business?

It depends on your state and industry. Workers' comp is mandatory almost everywhere once you have employees, commercial auto is required for business vehicles, and general liability — while not always legally mandatory — is usually required by leases and client contracts.

What is a Business Owners Policy?

A BOP bundles general liability and commercial property into one policy, typically at a discount versus buying each separately, often with business interruption coverage included.

Do I need commercial auto, or is my personal policy enough?

If a vehicle is registered to your business or used mainly for business, you need commercial auto. Personal auto policies explicitly exclude business use — a claim would be denied.

What is a Certificate of Insurance (COI) and how do I get one?

A COI is proof of active coverage that landlords, clients, or general contractors often require before you can work on their property or sign a contract. As your broker, we can issue one quickly.
